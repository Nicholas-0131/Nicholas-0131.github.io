一般波动方程（续）

对于$n=2k$为偶数的情况，我们首先看看$n=2$怎么办.首先，把$\mathbb{R}^2$嵌入到$\mathbb{R}^3$，具体把$\boldsymbol{x}$变成$\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}=(\boldsymbol{x},0)$.然后我们可以把$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)$也变成一个$\bar{u}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}},t)$，同理定义$\bar{g},\bar{h}$.不难看出，$\bar{u}$是三维Laplace方程有初值$g,h$的解.这样利用上面的公式，我们得到$\bar{u}(\bar{x},t)$的表达式. 接下来，我们利用投影方法，比较重要的一点是，点$\bar{y}=(\boldsymbol{y},z)\in\partial\bar{B}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}},t)$其实是指$z^2+|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2=t^2$，从而外法向为$\boldsymbol{\nu}=(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x},z)/t$.从而$\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}=|\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_3|\text{d}\bar{S}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}}=\frac{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}{t}\text{d}\bar{S}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}}$. 从而得到了$n=2$时候的解为

$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{t}{2}\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{g(\boldsymbol{y})+(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\mathbf{D}g(\boldsymbol{y})+h(\boldsymbol{y})t}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}.$$

利用同样的方法可以把所有$n=2k$为偶数的情况算出来，答案为

$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{1}{n!!}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{(n-2)/2}\left(t^n\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{g(\boldsymbol{y})}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{(n-2)/2}\left(t^n\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{h(\boldsymbol{y})}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}\right)\right].$$

从而，我们求出了齐次波动方程的解的表达式，更进一步，我们发现对于$n\geq3$的奇数来说，数据$g,h$仅仅影响了锥面$\partial\{(\boldsymbol{y},t):t>0,|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}| < t\}=\{(\boldsymbol{y},t):t>0,|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|=t\}$上面的取值；但是在$n$为偶数的时候，影响了整个锥体.这说明，奇数维的时候，扰动沿着波前传播出去；而偶数维的时候，扰动则会继续影响.这便是Huygens原理.

对于非齐次情况（注意这时候我们一般设定$u|_{t=0}=u_t|_{t=0}=0$，如果不是的话，直接把它写成非齐次的解和一个齐次的解的和即可），我们需要做的就是利用Duhamel原理做积分即可，即如果$u(\boldsymbol{x},t;s)$满足波动方程，且有初值$u(\boldsymbol{x},s;s)=0,u_t(\boldsymbol{x},s;s)=f(\boldsymbol{x},s)$，那么$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\int_0^tu(\boldsymbol{x},t;s)\text{d}s$是非齐次波动方程的解.特别地，$n=3$的时候，这个解正是$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{y},t-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|)}{|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y},$$也就是物理中所谓推迟势.

现在考虑利用能量方法解决波动方程.考虑能量定义为$$E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_Uw_t^2+|\mathbf{D}w|^2\text{d}\boldsymbol{x},$$然后我们假设$w$是边值条件为$0$的齐次方程的解，那么$$\dot{E}(t)=\int_Uw_tw_{tt}+\mathbf{D}w\cdot\mathbf{D}w_t\text{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\int_Uw_t(w_{tt}-\Delta w)\text{d}\boldsymbol{x}=0.$$从而$E(t)\equiv E(0)=0$，于是只能是$w=0$.这再次证明了初值问题解是唯一的. 更进一步，考虑波动方程的解的依赖区域，即$(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)$到底和哪些位置的点有关系，我们猜想这个点只与平面上$B(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)\times\{t=0\}$有关系，但是我们其实有更强的结论，那就是这个圆盘事实上决定了整个$K(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)=\{(\boldsymbol{x},t):t\in[0,t_0],|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_0|\leq t_0-t\}$.

至此，我们完整地回顾了第二章里面重要的内容。后面的内容将会根据我的需要和理解，进行较大程度的“从心所欲”的改编。

线性PDE的理论

首先讨论一下各种函数空间.在PDE中用的多的有两种：H$\boldsymbol{\ddot{\text{o}}}$lder空间和Sobolev空间.

所谓H$\ddot{\text{o}}$lder空间就是在$C^k(\bar{U})$当中且\[||u||_{C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{U})}=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\leq k}||\mathbf{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}u||_{C(\bar{U})}+\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=k}[\mathbf{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\bar{U})}\]有限的函数，其中\[[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\bar{U})}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in I}\left\{\frac{|u(\boldsymbol{x})-u(\boldsymbol{y})|}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{\gamma}}\right\}\]叫做H$\boldsymbol{\ddot{\text{o}}}$lder半范数.

Even-dimensional wave formulas, Huygens' principle, retarded potentials, and energy estimates; then a first look at Hölder spaces.

Even-dimensional waves: the method of descent

Start in two spatial dimensions and extend $u(x_1,x_2,t)$ to three by making it independent of $x_3$. Extend $g,h$ in the same way. The extended function solves the three-dimensional wave equation, so Kirchhoff's formula applies.

Project the upper and lower hemispheres onto the disk $B(x,t)$. On the sphere, $z^2+|y-x|^2=t^2$, and its outward unit normal is $(y-x,z)/t$. Hence $$dy=\frac{\sqrt{t^2-|y-x|^2}}t\,dS.$$ Accounting for both hemispheres yields the two-dimensional formula $$u(x,t)=\frac1{2\pi t}\int_{B(x,t)} \frac{g(y)+(y-x)\cdot Dg(y)+t h(y)}{\sqrt{t^2-|y-x|^2}}\,dy.$$ Unlike the three-dimensional formula, it integrates over a disk, not just its boundary.

For any even $n\geq2$, write $$A_tf(x)=\frac1{|B(x,t)|}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{f(y)}{\sqrt{t^2-|y-x|^2}}\,dy.$$ Descending from dimension $n+1$ gives $$u(x,t)=\frac1{n!!}\left[ \partial_t\left(\frac1t\partial_t\right)^{(n-2)/2}\big(t^nA_tg(x)\big) +\left(\frac1t\partial_t\right)^{(n-2)/2}\big(t^nA_th(x)\big)\right].$$ Here $n!!=n(n-2)\cdots2$.

Huygens' principle

For odd $n\geq3$, the value at $(x,t)$ depends only on the initial data on the sphere $\partial B(x,t)$, including the derivatives appearing in the representation formula. For even $n$, it depends on data throughout $B(x,t)$. Thus odd-dimensional waves have a sharp wave front, while even-dimensional waves leave a tail. This is the distinction expressed by the strong Huygens principle. Both cases still have finite propagation speed.

Sources and retarded potentials

For $u_{tt}-\Delta u=f$ with zero initial position and velocity, solve, for each $s$, a homogeneous wave equation for $v(x,t;s)$ with $$v(x,s;s)=0,\qquad v_t(x,s;s)=f(x,s).$$ Duhamel's principle gives $u(x,t)=\int_0^t v(x,t;s)\,ds$. In three dimensions this becomes $$u(x,t)=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{f(y,t-|y-x|)}{|y-x|}\,dy.$$ This is the retarded potential: the source is evaluated at the earlier time needed for a disturbance to travel from $y$ to $x$. For nonzero initial data, add the homogeneous solution.

Energy, uniqueness, and finite propagation

For a homogeneous wave solution $w$ with zero spatial boundary values, define $$E(t)=\frac12\int_U\big(w_t^2+|Dw|^2\big)\,dx.$$ Under the regularity assumptions permitting integration by parts, $$E'(t)=\int_U\big(w_tw_{tt}+Dw\cdot Dw_t\big)\,dx =\int_Uw_t(w_{tt}-\Delta w)\,dx=0.$$ If both initial data vanish, $E(0)=0$, so $w_t=Dw=0$; the initial displacement then fixes the constant as zero. Taking differences proves uniqueness.

The localized version uses a shrinking ball $B(x_0,t_0-t)$. Its energy derivative has a boundary contribution $$E_K'(t)=\int_{\partial B(x_0,t_0-t)} \left[w_t\partial_\nu w-\tfrac12\big(w_t^2+|Dw|^2\big)\right]dS\leq0,$$ because $2w_t\partial_\nu w\leq w_t^2+|Dw|^2$. Therefore initial data on $B(x_0,t_0)$ determine the solution throughout the backward cone $$K(x_0,t_0)=\{(x,t):0\leq t\leq t_0,\ |x-x_0|\leq t_0-t\}.$$ This energy argument explains the domain of dependence without requiring an explicit solution formula.

This completes the review of the main classical equations in Chapter 2. Subsequent reading will follow the topics most useful for my current interests.

Beginning the theory of linear PDEs

Two central families of function spaces in PDE theory are Hölder spaces and Sobolev spaces. This note starts with the former. For $0<\gamma\leq1$, define the Hölder seminorm $$[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline U)}= \sup_{\substack{x,y\in\overline U\\x\ne y}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^\gamma}.$$ The exclusion $x\ne y$ is necessary to avoid division by zero.

The space $C^{k,\gamma}(\overline U)$ consists of functions with continuous derivatives through order $k$ for which $$\|u\|_{C^{k,\gamma}(\overline U)}= \sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^\alpha u\|_{C(\overline U)} +\sum_{|\alpha|=k}[D^\alpha u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline U)}$$ is finite. The first sum controls the size of the derivatives; the second controls how rapidly the highest derivatives vary. The seminorm alone vanishes on constants, which is why the full norm includes the supremum terms.

一般の波動方程式（続き）

偶数次元$n=2k$について、まず$n=2$を考えます。$\mathbb{R}^2$を$\mathbb{R}^3$に埋め込み、具体的には$\boldsymbol{x}$を$\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}=(\boldsymbol{x},0)$に移します。同様に$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)$を$\bar{u}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}},t)$に拡張し、$\bar{g},\bar{h}$も定義します。$\bar{u}$は初期値$g,h$を持つ三次元の方程式の解となるので、前の公式から$\bar{u}(\bar{x},t)$の表示が得られます。次に射影を使います。点$\bar{y}=(\boldsymbol{y},z)\in\partial\bar{B}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}},t)$は$z^2+|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2=t^2$を満たすため、外向き単位法線は$\boldsymbol{\nu}=(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x},z)/t$であり、$\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}=|\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_3|\text{d}\bar{S}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}}=\frac{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}{t}\text{d}\bar{S}_{\bar{\boldsymbol{y}}}$となります。したがって、$n=2$の場合の解は次のとおりです。

$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{t}{2}\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{g(\boldsymbol{y})+(\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\mathbf{D}g(\boldsymbol{y})+h(\boldsymbol{y})t}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}.$$

同じ方法で、すべての偶数次元$n=2k$を計算できます。結果は次のとおりです。

$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{1}{n!!}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{(n-2)/2}\left(t^n\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{g(\boldsymbol{y})}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{(n-2)/2}\left(t^n\frac{1}{|B(\boldsymbol{x},t)|}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{h(\boldsymbol{y})}{\sqrt{t^2-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|^2}}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y}\right)\right].$$

これで斉次波動方程式の解の表示が得られました。奇数$n\geq3$では、初期データ$g,h$のうち、円錐面$\partial\{(\boldsymbol{y},t):t>0,|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}| < t\}=\{(\boldsymbol{y},t):t>0,|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|=t\}$上の値だけが影響します。一方、$n$が偶数の場合は円錐内部全体が影響します。奇数次元では擾乱は波面に沿って伝わり、偶数次元ではその後も影響が残るということです。これがホイヘンスの原理です。

非斉次の場合には、デュアメルの原理によって積分すればよいことになります。通常は$u|_{t=0}=u_t|_{t=0}=0$とし、そうでない場合には非斉次問題の解と斉次問題の解に分けます。$u(\boldsymbol{x},t;s)$が初期値$u(\boldsymbol{x},s;s)=0,u_t(\boldsymbol{x},s;s)=f(\boldsymbol{x},s)$を持つ波動方程式の解なら、$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\int_0^tu(\boldsymbol{x},t;s)\text{d}s$は非斉次波動方程式の解です。特に$n=3$では$$u(\boldsymbol{x},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{x},t)}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{y},t-|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|)}{|\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{x}|}\text{d}\boldsymbol{y},$$となり、物理学でいう遅延ポテンシャルに対応します。

次にエネルギー法を考え、エネルギーを$$E(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_Uw_t^2+|\mathbf{D}w|^2\text{d}\boldsymbol{x},$$と定義します。$w$が境界条件$0$を持つ斉次方程式の解なら$$\dot{E}(t)=\int_Uw_tw_{tt}+\mathbf{D}w\cdot\mathbf{D}w_t\text{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\int_Uw_t(w_{tt}-\Delta w)\text{d}\boldsymbol{x}=0.$$なので、$E(t)\equiv E(0)=0$、したがって$w=0$です。これで初期値問題の解の一意性が再び示されます。さらに、解の依存領域、つまり点$(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)$がどの点のデータに依存するかを考えます。この点は平面上の$B(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)\times\{t=0\}$にのみ依存すると予想できますが、実際にはさらに強く、この円盤は$K(\boldsymbol{x}_0,t_0)=\{(\boldsymbol{x},t):t\in[0,t_0],|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_0|\leq t_0-t\}$全体を決定します。

ここまでで第2章の重要な内容を一通り復習しました。これ以降は、自分の必要と理解に合わせて、構成をかなり自由に組み替えていく予定です。

線形偏微分方程式の理論

まず関数空間について考えます。偏微分方程式でよく使うのは、H$\boldsymbol{\ddot{\text{o}}}$lder（ヘルダー）空間とソボレフ空間です。

H$\ddot{\text{o}}$lder（ヘルダー）空間は、$C^k(\bar{U})$に属し、さらに\[||u||_{C^{k,\gamma}(\bar{U})}=\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|\leq k}||\mathbf{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}u||_{C(\bar{U})}+\sum_{|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=k}[\mathbf{D}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\bar{U})}\]が有限である関数からなります。ここで\[[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(\bar{U})}=\sup_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in I}\left\{\frac{|u(\boldsymbol{x})-u(\boldsymbol{y})|}{|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{y}|^{\gamma}}\right\}\]をH$\boldsymbol{\ddot{\text{o}}}$lder（ヘルダー）半ノルムと呼びます。
